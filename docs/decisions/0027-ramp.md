# 0027 — Ramp

**Status:** decided, 2026-11-14 · **Milestone:** 0.8

## Question

Fusion's Ramp finishes walls as its Contour does, but going down them without
stopping, round and round, rather than level by level. Autodesk's reference
says only that it "steps down the walls a specified Z distance for each
complete revolution" and that its Ramping Angle is "the maximum ramping angle
of the helix". How far down does it go each time round a wall that is not
upright, where does it start and stop, and what is a wall when a part has more
than one?

## What was found

A fresh Ramp read from Dan's Fusion through its API (`params/ramp.json`) is a
fresh Contour (`contour3d`) with a hidden `rampMode` on, and these different:
Tool Containment outside the boundary (Contour's is on its centre), no
additional offset, a safe distance of 2 mm, and its Shaft and Holder, off,
pulling away where Contour's trims. It
has no Order by Depth or by Islands, no Rest Machining, Repeat Finishing Pass,
Thin Wall or transition, and no Smooth Ramps. It starts with Direction climb,
Maximum Stepdown 1 mm, Flat Area Detection on, Machine Shallow Areas off, Order
Bottom-Up off, Stock to Leave off, Fillets off, Slope off (0° to 90°), Minimum
Diameter and Minimum Cutting Radius none, tolerance 0.01 mm, a full retraction,
the Top from the stock's top and the Bottom from the model's bottom; its
Linking tab's Ramping Angle is the tool's, 2° for the sample library's tools,
with its Ramp Type helix, Maximum Ramp Stepdown the stepdown and Ramp Clearance
Height 1.05 stepdowns.

`fixtures/step/ops_3d.step` was made for this and the 3D finishing to come
(`scripts/fusion/ops_3d/part.py`): a 60 x 40 x 20 block, a pocket 10 deep with
one wall drafted 15°, one corner R5 and three sharp, and R4 fillets round its floor,
a dome 5 tall on the floor, and the block's top edge at +X rounded R8. Fusion's
Ramp with the sample library's 6 mm ball was posted for it as a new one
starts, and again with one setting changed at a time: Maximum Stepdown 1.5 and
2 mm, Ramping Angle 10° and 0.5°, conventional, Order Bottom-Up, the leads off,
Flat Area Detection off (at 1 and at 1.5 mm), Ramp Type zig-zag, profile and
plunge, ramping off, minimum retraction, Machine Shallow Areas, a slope range,
stock to leave, a flat and a bull nose tool, the stock flush with the part,
the Top 1 mm higher and 0.5 mm lower, and the tool's centre on the boundary.
The part was first made with the drafted wall's corners rounded too; Fusion
blended those into the floor's fillets with B-spline faces it wrote 0.0096 mm
off their edges, which camcraft's importer refuses finer than 0.04 mm, so the
committed part has them sharp. Most of the findings below were measured on
both; where only one, it says. Four scratch parts besides (a block; a
cylinder and a cone standing on a base; a block with a round pocket) settled
which walls are gone round at their top and in what order. What the programs
do:

- **The rings are Contour's.** At every height the tool is where it first
  touches the part, as worked out from the model: within 0.001 mm on the
  outside walls, and within 0.013 mm on the dome, its points a hundredth
  inside the circle there, as a polygon's corners round a circle are to
  keep its chords on it. The levels are a stepdown apart from the Top, the
  last at the Bottom, and with Flat Area Detection one at the pocket's floor:
  at 1.5 mm the pocket's wall ends at Z10, its floor, and at Z11 without.
- **A wall is a pass.** Each wall is one pass from its top to its foot,
  lifted to the retract height between. Where rings part going down, as the
  cylinder's ring gives way to its base's, or a ring is broken open (the
  rounded edge, with the tool's centre kept to the boundary, or with a slope
  range), the walls end: the open stretches are cut at their level, each its
  own pass, after the walls.
- **Into a wall.** Each is entered from the level above its top ring, or the
  Top for the first level, along that ring at its XY, down to its level. The
  pocket's first ring is at Z19 and its way down starts at Z20; the dome's at
  Z14, from Z15, the level above.
- **Where.** On the committed part the pocket starts exactly at its ring's
  lower left corner, (-23.762, -10.764), and the dome at 227° round its
  centre, where the point nearest its ring's lower left is 225°; the outside
  starts 200° round its corner arc, 25° short of that.
- **Once round its top, but on the first wall.** Every wall but the first one
  cut goes once round its top ring at its level before going on down: the
  pocket and the dome, not the outside; the cylinder's base and not the
  cylinder; and with the outside left out (the tool's centre on the boundary),
  the dome and not the pocket. Nothing else the probes changed (the leads, the
  stock, the Top, the ramp type, the angle) changed this.
- **Down the wall.** The tool falls at no more than the Ramping Angle, and no
  more than a stepdown each time round the longer of the two rings it goes
  between. Round the outside, whose rings grow going down, each step took the
  lower ring's length, as Fusion's Contour has it at the same levels, to
  within 0.3%: once round. In the pocket, whose rings shrink, the first step
  took 106.15 mm to the upper ring's 106.46, and on the committed part each lap
  of 100 to 106 mm fell 0.995 to 1.000 mm. On the dome the lower ring is far
  the longer (Z14's ring is 27.9 mm round, Z13's 39.0): the step from Z14 to
  Z13 took 39.0 mm, more than once round, and the first, from Z15, went at
  2.000°, the ramp angle, where a stepdown in Z14's ring would be 2.05°. At
  0.5° every wall but the outside's went at the angle.
- **Once round its foot.** Each wall ends once round its last ring at its
  level: the outside at Z0, the pocket and the dome at Z10.
- **Which wall next.** The nearest. Where there was a choice, after the
  outside the pocket came before the dome, on both versions of the part, and
  on the committed part the pocket's last wall, from Z11, before the dome,
  from Z15: not the highest first. The first wall of each of the seven
  programs whose order says anything (16 walls) was the one starting highest,
  the cylinder and the cone before their bases, and of the outside and the
  pocket, both from Z20, the larger.
- **The pocket's wall ends above its floor.** On the committed part, at a 1 mm
  stepdown, the pocket's wall ends at Z11, once round there, and a wall of its
  own goes from Z11 down Z10's ring, once round it; on the first version of
  the part it went on down to Z10. On the committed part it ended above the
  floor at stepdowns of 0.9 mm (at Z10.5), 1 mm (Z11), 1.1 mm (Z10.6) and 2 mm
  (Z12), and with the Top 0.8 mm lower (Z10.6); it did not at 0.5, 0.8, 1.2,
  1.25 or 1.5 mm, nor with the Top 0.2 or 0.5 mm lower. It follows neither how
  far the last level is above the floor nor whether the floor is a level of
  its own. What makes it was not found.

Order Bottom-Up, Machine Shallow Areas and stock to leave each changed more than
the order or the levels (stock to leave broke the outside into a helix to Z18
and level rings on the rounded edge below), and are not taken here.

## What was decided

**`strategy::ramp`, on Contour's rings.** `waterline::passes_at_levels` now
gives the rings Contour cuts, at the same levels, kept to the area, pulled onto
the part and turned as asked, and both strategies take them from there.
Contour's own paths are unchanged.

**Its region at three tolerances.** Contour draws the region its rings come
from at ten tolerances, as every point is pulled onto the part anyway
([decision 0010](0010-contour-and-flat.md)). Ramp needs the region's rings
themselves to be right: which rings there are is what says where a wall goes
on down. At ten, the 0.11 mm between the dome's foot and the pocket's fillets
at Z10 closed, which joined the dome to the walls and parted the pocket in two,
and with a 2 mm stepdown the walls followed into one another and cut the part
0.68 mm deep. At three the gap stays open and the rings at Z10 are Fusion's
(the pocket's 91.94 mm round to Fusion's 92.22, the dome's 55.84 to its 55.82);
finding the rings took 22 s, against 117 s at one tolerance, in a test build.

**Walls by containment.** A ring round a piece of the part (the part on the
inside of it) is followed by the ring below round the piece that holds it, a
point just inside the part beside it; a ring round a hole by the ring above
round the hole that holds it, a point just outside. The part only grows going
down, so a piece is always in one below and a hole in one above: this needs no
distances or thresholds, and identical rings on upright walls are followed as
surely as growing ones. Where pieces meet going down, the one whose ring is
nearest the ring below goes on down it, as the outside does round a boss whose
foot meets it, and the others end; where a hole parts in two, its wall ends.

**Down each wall as Fusion's goes.** From the level above (the Top for the
first level) along the top ring, at the gentler of the ramp angle and a
stepdown in that ring's length, from the ring's point nearest the lower left of
the box round it; once round it at its level unless it is the first wall cut,
or the wall's only ring; then each ring below gone down to from the one above,
falling at the gentler of the ramp angle and a stepdown in the longer of the
two rings, measured along the way the tool goes; and once round the last ring
at its level. The lower ring is clear of the part at every height above it, as
the part's shadow only grows going down, so each of its points is pulled in
towards the part at the height the tool is at there, until the tool first
touches it, looking no further than the ring above; a point not found within
that is left on the lower ring, clear, and said (CC215). Between each two, the
chord's middle is pulled onto the part at its height, looking as far as half
the chord either way, and kept where the chord strays more than a quarter of
the tolerance, as Contour's are; then each point's height is worked out along
the lap as cut, chords and all, the point put on the part there, and the chords
kept on it again. Measured along the ring's own points only, the heights fell
5% too slowly in the pocket, whose chords the refinement lengthens; looked for
no further than a level ring's chords are, a chord across the rounded edge,
its two ends at different heights, cut 0.08 mm into the wall.

**Which wall next.** The nearest; the first, of those starting highest, the
largest. Open stretches are
cut at their level after the walls, as Contour cuts them. Where Fusion ends the
pocket's wall above its floor is not followed: camcraft goes on down.

**Settings.** `direction` (climb or conventional), `flat_areas` and the slopes,
after Fusion's; the stepdown is `passes.stepdown`, a millimetre to start, and
the angle `linking.ramp_angle`, Fusion's Ramping Angle on its Linking tab. A new
Ramp keeps the tool outside the silhouette, as Fusion's, and lifts to the
retract height between walls; it never stays down, as Fusion's programs did
not. Its ramp is its own, so the linker comes down onto each wall's start
along its vertical arc and does not ramp into it.

## Held to Fusion

`crates/camcraft-session/src/compare/tests_ramp.rs` roughs the part with a new
adaptive clearing and a 6 mm flat, leaving half a millimetre, then makes a new
Ramp, one with a 2 mm stepdown and one with a 0.5° ramp angle, and cuts each of
Fusion's three programs from the material the roughing left, with the same
ball. Neither program cuts the part, camcraft's rapids meet nothing, both go
as deep, and:

| Program      | Columns cut | Alike  | Median   | 95th     | Removed | Fed    |
|--------------|------------:|-------:|---------:|---------:|--------:|-------:|
| `ramp`       |     690,830 | 61.7%  | 0.005 mm | 0.31 mm  | +0.6%   | -1.5%  |
| `ramp_step2` |     663,776 | 46.2%  | 0.012 mm | 0.82 mm  | +1.8%   | +5.8%  |
| `ramp_angle` |     691,354 | 60.6%  | 0.006 mm | 0.32 mm  | +0.7%   | -1.4%  |

The test holds each to a little worse than that. It is far from the 2D
operations' column in a thousand. The outside's upright walls agree all the
way down; the differences are on the pocket's rim and floor fillets, round the
dome and on the rounded edge. Where they come from:

- **Where Fusion's pocket wall ends above the floor.** Fusion goes once round
  at Z11 (Z12 at 2 mm) and comes down Z10's ring on its own; camcraft's helix
  goes on down the floor's fillets and into the half millimetre the roughing
  left on the floor, a band of columns lower all round the pocket. At 2 mm the
  band is two levels deep, which is most of what that program's figures lose.
- **Cusps.** A helix leaves cusps on the curved ground (the rim's edge, the
  fillets, the dome, the rounded edge), a tenth of a millimetre or so with a
  6 mm ball a millimetre a lap. Where they fall follows where each lap starts
  and how fast it falls, which are Fusion's to the extent this decision says:
  the pocket starts at Fusion's point and falls within 0.5% as fast, the
  outside starts 25° round its corner from Fusion's, and Fusion's Z10 ring in
  the pocket, where the ball rests on the floor between the fillets, lies
  0.05 mm nearer the walls than camcraft's.
- **Fusion's horizontal leads**, an arc a tenth of the tool onto each wall and
  off it, which camcraft does not make.

Fusion's 0.5° program rapids down to Z12 at (-15.36, 7.83), onto the wall it
starts from Z11.6 in the pocket, into the stock this test's roughing leaves
round the dome's foot, 3.37 mm deep; the test allows Fusion that one.

## Not yet

Fusion's horizontal leads; where Fusion ends a pocket's wall above its floor;
where it starts the outside; Order Bottom-Up; Machine Shallow Areas; Minimum
Diameter and Minimum Cutting Radius; Fillets; Contact Point Boundary; Ramp Type
(every type but zig-zag cut the same programs; zig-zag split off the pocket's
last lap). Stock to leave, slope ranges, conventional cutting and flat or bull
nose tools are offered but not compared with Fusion's programs. A new Ramp of
`ops_3d` runs in 86 s in a test build, its simulation and checks included, of
which finding the rings is about 22 s.
