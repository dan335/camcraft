# 0019 — Undo: what it covers, and making it whole

**Status:** built, 2026-11-05, but for the history list · **Milestone:** 0.6

## Where it stands

Each open job keeps its own undo (`Document::{checkpoint, undo, redo}`):

- **The step is the whole job.** Before an edit the job is kept whole, up to
  200 of them. Undo puts the last one back; redo the one undone.
- **An edit is finished when nothing is typed in or dragged.** At the end of
  each frame where no field has the keyboard and nothing is dragged, a job
  that differs from the last kept one becomes a step. So typing a number is
  one step, not one per key, and a drag is one step.
- **What a run made follows the job.** Passes, keyframes and layouts are kept
  by what they are worked out from ([decision 0017](0017-what-a-run-keeps.md)),
  so an undone edit finds its passes again rather than generating afresh.
  A renamed setup keeps its run, view and selection through undo and redo
  (`follow_renames`).
- **Keys and menu:** Ctrl+Z, Ctrl+Shift+Z, and *Edit ▸ Undo / Redo*, greyed
  out when there is nothing to undo or redo.

Whole-job steps are simple and cannot miss a field, which is why they stay.

## What is wrong or missing

1. **Worked-out settings make steps of their own.** Expressions are worked out
   at the start of the next frame. An edit to a tool's diameter is one step,
   and the stepover its expression then changes is a second. Undo takes back
   the stepover alone; the expression puts it straight back, which is a new
   edit and clears redo. Undo seems to do nothing.
2. **The tool library has no undo.** Its edits are saved to the library file
   as they finish, so a tool or folder deleted there is gone.
3. **A step does not say what it undoes.** The menu reads *Undo*, never
   *Undo stepover of rough*.
4. **Undo does not show where.** A step in another setup, or on a tab not
   showing, changes it out of sight.
5. **Two fields edited without leaving the keyboard are one step.** Tab from
   one field to the next and the keyboard is never free, so both edits undo
   together.
6. **No Ctrl+Y**, the redo most Windows and Linux programs have.

## Plan

In order; each is its own commit, with tests.

1. **What follows an edit is part of it.** A checkpoint works the job's
   expressions out before it compares, so a step is kept with what it caused
   (`Document::checkpoint`). A job read from a file is worked out as it is
   read (`parse_job`), so opening one is no edit. Test: change a tool's
   diameter, undo once, and the diameter and stepover are both back and stay
   back; redo, and both come again.
2. **A step per field.** Checkpoint when the field with the keyboard changes
   as well as when none has it: the focused widget's id is remembered from
   frame to frame. Test: two fields typed into with Tab between are two
   steps.
3. **Ctrl+Y redoes**, as Ctrl+Shift+Z does, and the menu shows both keys.
4. **Steps say what they undo.** A step is named by comparing the two jobs as
   job files, by the settings' places that differ, as the panels' defaults
   already compare them (`job::settings`): one setting reads as its label and
   the operation (*stepover of rough*); a thing added, removed or renamed as
   that (*add operation finish*, *delete setup bottom*); several as the thing
   edited (*edit rough*). Kept beside each step, shown as *Undo …* and
   *Redo …* in the menu and on hover.
5. **Undo goes to where it changed.** After undo or redo, the setup, the
   operation and, where one setting changed, the tab holding it are shown, so
   what changed is in front of you.
6. **The tool library gets the same undo.** The manager keeps whole-library
   steps as the job does, checkpointed the same way, with Ctrl+Z and
   Ctrl+Shift+Z while its window has focus and *Undo* and *Redo* buttons in
   it. Undoing writes the library again, as any edit does. A tool or folder
   deleted can be got back.
7. **Later, if wanted: a history list.** The named steps in a list under
   *Edit ▸ History*, a click going back or forward to that step.

## As built

- Steps are named by `document::history::change`, comparing the two jobs as
  job files. A setting only written because another in its section changed,
  at its default, is not counted; nor is one an expression works out, since
  the expression or what it reads was the edit.
- Where a step shows is found in the job as it is after undoing or redoing,
  so a rename undone selects the setup by its old name.
- While the tool library's window is open, the keys undo its edits rather
  than the job's.

## Left as it is

- **Whole-job steps**, not edits recorded one by one: a job is small, 200 of
  them are a few megabytes, and a snapshot cannot miss a field a recorded edit
  forgot. If a job with many picks makes this heavy, the jobs can share their
  unchanged setups (`Arc`) rather than change how steps work.
- **Nothing outside the job and the library**: the view, the camera, the
  playhead, what is selected and the app's settings are not edits to undo.
- **History ends with the window.** It is not saved with the job.
